# Are the score's weights defensible?

Generated by `python -m src.weights`. The proposal fixes the weights at 0.40 / 0.35 / 0.25 by judgement. This is the check that they are not arbitrary, following the OECD/JRC *Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators* (2008), which treats a stated weighting scheme plus an uncertainty and sensitivity analysis as the minimum standard for a composite index.

## Alternative weighting schemes

| Scheme | Failure risk | Error freq | Consistency | PR-AUC | Rank agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| proposal (0.40/0.35/0.25) | 0.40 | 0.35 | 0.25 | 0.968 | 1.000 |
| equal (1/3 each) | 0.33 | 0.33 | 0.33 | 0.959 | 0.978 |
| entropy | 0.92 | 0.04 | 0.04 | 0.982 | 0.803 |
| discriminative | 0.93 | 0.06 | 0.02 | 0.983 | 0.722 |
| learned (logistic) | 0.81 | 0.09 | 0.10 | 0.980 | 0.900 |

*Rank agreement* is the mean Spearman correlation between that scheme's ordering of APIs and the proposal's, computed within the same window -- the operational question, since an engineer reads the ordering of APIs at one moment rather than the absolute number.

## What the data says the weights should be

Every data-driven scheme concentrates weight on the failure-risk term and all but removes the other two. That is consistent with the component separation measured in `ABLATIONS.md`: failure risk separates failing from healthy windows by about 25 standard deviations, error frequency by 3.4, and response consistency by 0.44. Weighting a 0.44-sigma signal at 0.25 alongside a 25-sigma signal at 0.40 is what makes the composite rank slightly worse than the risk term alone.

## Uncertainty analysis

400 weight vectors were drawn from a flat Dirichlet distribution and the score recomputed for each.

| | PR-AUC |
|---|---|
| Worst random weighting | 0.173 |
| 5th percentile | 0.367 |
| Mean | 0.830 |
| 95th percentile | 0.980 |
| Best random weighting | 0.983 |
| **The proposal's weights** | **0.968** (70% percentile) |

The spread across *every* weighting from pure-risk to pure-consistency is 0.810 PR-AUC. That is wide, so the choice of weights materially changes the result and must be justified rather than assumed.

## Conclusion

The proposal's weights sit at the 70% percentile of random weightings, so they are a reasonable rather than an unlucky choice, but they are **not** the accuracy-maximising ones -- discriminative reaches 0.983. Re-weighting towards the failure-risk term would improve ranking.

That improvement should be declined, and the reason is the point of this analysis. Fitting weights to maximise ranking accuracy turns the composite into a second, worse classifier -- the model already performs that task, and performs it better. The score's purpose is to give an operator a number they can decompose into *why*: how much of this API's poor score is predicted risk, how much is errors it is actually returning, how much is unstable latency. The honest position is that 0.40 / 0.35 / 0.25 is a **presentation** choice, now measured rather than assumed, with the cost of that choice quantified here and in `ABLATIONS.md`.

A project that needed the weights to be optimal rather than interpretable should use the `learned` row, and say so.
